Technology Does Not End Scarcity — It Relocates It

Every technological revolution tells the same story: it turns a scarcity into abundance. Fire tamed darkness, electricity tamed night, communication tamed distance, chips tamed computation, the internet tamed copying. The story usually stops here and starts celebrating.

It never tells the second half: abundance is never free. Every abundance pushes scarcity up one layer.

After bandwidth became abundant, attention became scarce — in an age of infinite information, the day still lasts twenty-four hours. After information became abundant, trust became scarce — telling true from false cost, for the first time, more than obtaining it. After compute became abundant, questions became scarce — answers got so cheap that no one asks what a question is worth. After copying became abundant, original creation became scarce — in the age of infinite copies, only the uncopyable holds value.

The rule is a single line: technology relocates scarcity — from the material layer to the information layer, from information to cognition, and in the end to meaning.

So to grade a technology, don't count the abundance it produces; watch which layer it pushes scarcity onto. The steam engine moved scarcity from muscle to energy. The internet moved it from access to filtering. AI is moving it from answers to questions.

The ultimate question rewrites itself: when matter is fully abundant, scarcity remains in one place only — who you want to become. No technology can answer that for you.

Technology does not end scarcity. It finishes the move, and leaves the most expensive question standing where it was.
