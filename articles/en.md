Intuition Is Linear — the World Is Exponential

The human brain was calibrated for the savanna of ten thousand years ago. Everything on the savanna was linear: twice the distance, twice the time; twice the wolves, twice the trouble. Today's world is almost entirely exponential, while intuition never left the savanna.

So humans systematically underestimate every exponential process, without noticing.

Compound interest: at seven percent a year, money doubles in a decade. Intuition computes seventy percent over ten years — off by nearly half. Moore's law: fifty years, thirty doublings of transistors, a factor of a billion. Intuition only sees the last two doublings, which is why the prophecy "we're near the limit" appears every five years — and has been wrong for fifty.

Rice on a chessboard: one grain on the first square, doubling each square. The first half of the board is boring; the sixty-third square alone holds more than half the world's yearly harvest. Pandemics, compounding growth, compute, user networks — all the same board. Boredom through the first half, shock at the second, and never a warning in between.

The mechanism is a single line: the early part of an exponential looks like a straight line, and intuition only samples the early part. By the time you can see the curve bend, only the last few squares of doubling remain.

The answer is not to fix intuition — a ten-thousand-year factory setting cannot be rewritten. The answer is to convert: think in doubling times instead of speeds, in logarithmic scales instead of linear ones, in extrapolation instead of feel. The rule of seventy-two is not a math trick; it is a crutch for linear animals.

Underestimating exponentials is not a mistake; it is the default. Those who know this do not bet on seeing the inflection point. They bet the inflection point will come.

Intuition draws a straight line; the world runs on a curve. The winners simply switched to a logarithmic axis early.
