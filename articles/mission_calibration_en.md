# One Mission: Real Problems Are the Only Calibrator

Civilization's disease is not a shortage of technology. It is technology aimed at fake problems.

Fake problems share three marks. Solve one, and nobody hurts. It sits on no particular person — it has no address. And it exists for its own sake: born for a paper, born for funding, born for a demo. Fake problems feed large organizations; the organizations then feed the fake problems back.

Real problems have three tests. Remove one, and someone hurts. It has a name and an address — it lands on particular people. Solve it, and time is returned: hours once spent working around it are taken back and spent on something else.

Science, technology, engineering are three stations of one line. Science discovers the laws. Technology amplifies them. Engineering delivers the amplified thing, stably, into particular hands. Missing any station, the problem stays unsolved: without science it is brute force; without technology, empty talk; without engineering, a demo. Most failures are not a shortage of intelligence — they are a missing station.

So the first mission is not a slogan. It is a calibrator. Topic selection, hiring, investment, today's hours — each can be put through the same test: which real problem does this solve? Whatever has no answer is manufacturing noise.

The cheaper the compute, the sharper this question. AGI pushes the marginal cost of production toward zero, and the output of fake problems explodes with it: overnight, a thousand plausible artifacts. The easier generation becomes, the more expensive the judgment of what is worth generating. The calibrator appreciates.

Sixteen visions begin here. However high the ladder, the first rung is this one foundation: use science, technology, and engineering to solve real problems.

One point becomes the way; the way becomes a life; a life becomes a lineage.
